Living quantities

A sketch, placed by hand from the values of the Queue scene in cells.html (capacity 2, nine steps). Nothing here is derived yet: it fixes the language before the fold draws it.

1. A place holds its quantity as units

The buffer is a vessel with one slot for each unit of its capacity: a hollow slot is room (promised), a droplet is a message held (kept). An offer that finds no room waits at the inlet as a parked request holding its message; a taker that finds nothing waits at the outlet with empty hands. The capacity, the count held, the offers waiting and the takers waiting are four numbers, read here as one picture: how full, how much pressure, how much demand. A channel's width is what it carries in the step (da Vinci's rule: the width of a branch is its share of the flow); idle, it is the finest line.

2. A step moves units along the channels

Take, request 4, on the full vessel with an offer waiting: the front droplet leaves through the outlet to the taker, the one behind slides to the front, and the waiting offer's droplet enters the room that opened. The ghosts are the state before; the paths are what moves in the beat (a body travels along a shallow arc and settles). Both channels carry one message, so both are one unit wide.

3. The run is one level

On one axis: takers waiting are a deficit below zero, held messages fill the vessel up to its brim, and offers waiting are pressure above it. In this profile one signed level decides all three, except while a wake is in flight (step 8: a message held and its taker woken, not yet gone). The level is drawn by the monotone curve, which passes through every count and never overshoots, so the flow reads as alive without inventing a quantity. What crosses the membrane in each beat enters at the top or leaves at the bottom, with its value; each bar line is one command.

4. Where the messages went

The totals of the run as channels whose widths add up: four offered, three entered at once and one waited at the inlet; four held in turn; three taken at once and one taken after its taker waited. The branches of a split sum to the trunk, as the messages are conserved.
