1. The Organism: Honest Lumen, Organic Collars, and Dual-Layer Transport

Algorithms: Murray's Law · Hagen–Poiseuille Dual Layer · Organic Collar Distance Field (Wyvill Refined)

Channel walls represent accumulated Physarum conductivity, while an active inner core conveys instantaneous step flux. Payloads translate through the lumen rather than leaping through the sky.

3. The Run as One Level: Fritsch–Carlson Monotone Spline with Signed Baseline Stratum

Algorithms: Fritsch–Carlson Monotone Spline (W19) · Signed Fluid Stratum · Murray Pulse Markers

The run's four quantities (held, capacity, waiting offers, waiting takers) are projected onto a single continuous signed vertical scale. Capacity defines a bounded baseline stratum $[0, 2]$. Held units form the internal volume. Offers waiting create positive pressure rising above capacity. Takers waiting create a negative deficit sinking below zero. The boundary is traced by a Fritsch–Carlson monotone cubic spline, guaranteeing zero overshoot: the line never invents a quantity that the discrete state machine does not hold.

4. Totals as Conserved Channels: Murray's Law and the da Vinci Pipe Model

Algorithms: Murray's Law ($r_0^3 = \sum r_i^3$) · Leonardo da Vinci Pipe Flow ($\sum w_i = w_0$) · Area-Preserving Ribbon Split

The run's aggregate flow is drawn as an organic branching and merging conduit network. Leonardo da Vinci's rule and Murray's minimal-work principle govern ribbon widths: the width of the trunk equals the sum of the branch widths. At the inlet, 4 offered units split into 3 direct admissions and 1 queued offer. At the outlet, 4 taken units split into 3 immediate takes and 1 served waiter. Flow is rigorously conserved across every junction.
