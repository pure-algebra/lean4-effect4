## Findings

### 1. A type has five forms, and one key joins them

The subject is the data of the to-do application: `Todo`, `NotFound`, `SqlError`,
`EmptyTitle`, and the exit of the program `complete`.

| Form | Declaration | How the probe reads it | Key |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The type | `Ty` (`src/Effect4/Program/TyCore.lean`) | its generic layer: `TyAlgebra.ofLayer` | `y:<type>:<path>` |
| The schema | `Representation` and `Check` (`src/Effect4/Schema/Representation.lean`) | one layer by a hand `match`, walked with fuel | `z:<type>:<path>` |
| The printed schema | `representationSource` (`src/Effect4/Codegen/Schema.lean`) | the text, with the span of each node of the type | a span carries `y:` |
| A value | `Store.Val`, with `Val.hasTy` and `Schema.encode` | membership, the JSON text and the value read back | none yet |
| A use | `Row` and the type of a built program | the columns of a row and of a program refer to the types they hold | `r:todo:<position>` |

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  T["type (Ty)"] -- "Schema.Bridge.schema" --> S["schema (Representation)"]
  S -- "ofSchema" --> T
  S -- "representationSource" --> P["printed schema (JSON text)"]
  T -- "Val.hasTy" --> V["value (Val)"]
  V -- "Schema.encode / decode" --> J["JSON"]
  R["row, program"] -- "a column holds" --> T
```

Two links are computed with no change to the tree, by one method.

- **Type node to schema node.** The export replaces the node at a path with a handle of a
  reserved name (`markAt`). The bridge writes a handle as one declaration. The path of that
  declaration in the schema is the image of the type's node.
- **Type node to printed span.** The export prints the schema of the marked type. It finds the
  object of the mark. The unmarked print holds the node's text between the same head and tail.

Tested, `schemas-run.txt`:

| Check | Of | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A node of a type has its node of the schema | 25 | 25 |
| A node of a type has its span in the printed schema | 25 | 25 |
| The schema of a type reads back as the same type (`ofSchema`) | 5 | 5 |
| The written type is the canonical type | 5 | 4 |

The 5 types have 25 nodes. Their schemas have 34 lines: 26 nodes and 8 checks.

### 4. The picture of a page has three forms

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  O["object: Eff, Ty, Representation, Run"] -- "export (Lines.lean, Schemas.lean)" --> G["Page: rows in cells, with keys"]
  G -- "pageOps" --> C["Op: a call of the painter, logical pixels"]
  C -- "lower" --> D["Dev: a call that reaches the target"]
  D -- "draw.c" --> X["cairo and pango: pixels"]
```

| Form | Declared in `Scene.lean` | Unit | Constructors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Page` | lines, spans, boxes and edges: the viewer's rows | cells | 4 kinds of row |
| `Op` | one call of `paint.h` | logical pixels at zoom 1 | 12: fill, two rules, frame, two edges, mark, two texts, cut, end of cut, pointer box |
| `Dev` | one call that reaches the target | whole device pixels for a fill | 6: fill, two texts, cut, end of cut, pointer box |

One fact makes the lowest form small. **Every mark that is no text is a fill of a box of whole
device pixels in the tone of a role.** A rule, a frame, a band, an edge of a tree and each
shape of the row marks all end in `paint_device_fill` (`paint.h`). So Lean decides every such
pixel in integers.

A text is the host's. Lean states its face, its place, its room and its characters. Pango
chooses the glyphs. For the data face, Lean also knows the cells that a text takes: one for
each character.

A device fill is three calls of cairo: `cairo_set_source_rgba`, `cairo_rectangle` and
`cairo_fill`. So the stream of `Dev` is the form of Cairo commands, less the shaping of text.

### 5. Lean's lowering writes the viewer's own stream

The viewer now writes the stream of its own picture (`view --streams`). A hook in `paint.h`
writes one row for each call that reaches the target. `Scene.lean` writes the same form from
the rows alone. `lower-check.sh` compares the two.

Tested, `lower-run.txt`, on the 125 pages of the four row files:

| Comparison | Of | Equal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The stream of a page: Lean's rows against the viewer's rows, under 8 settings | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| The picture of a page: the replay of Lean's stream against the viewer's picture, in bytes | 7 | 7 |

The settings are the column of types, the indent rules, no marks, and four selections. The
last run lowers 125 pages to 20,091 device calls, 3,617 of them fills, in 168 ms. Lean's
interpreter ran it.

`draw.c` has 117 lines. It holds no layout, no rule of a page and no meaning of a role beyond
the tokens. It replays a row through the function of `paint.h` that the row names.

The reach of this result is a fragment: a whole page, at zoom 1, at the ratio 2, with no
cursor and no hidden line.

### 6. The pointer: one law, and two defects of the viewer

A page lowers to pointer boxes too. A row `H key x0 y0 x1 y1` is a box of device pixels that
answers a pointer with a key. `pick` is the meaning: the last box that holds the pixel answers.
A line answers with its key. A span answers before its line, and the narrowest span is last.

The viewer's click was compared with `pick` on a grid of 24,156 points a page.

- Before the fixes, 3 of 5 pages differed, at 2,250, 153 and 45 points
  (`lower-before-fix.txt`).
- **Defect 1.** Two spans of one extent stood on one line. The viewer took the first after an
  unstable sort. So the sort decided which node a click selects.
- **Defect 2.** The canvas's pick held a box's right and bottom edge. A click answered one
  pixel outside the fill.

Both are fixed in C. A box holds `[x, x + w)` by `[top, top + height)`, as its fill does. Of
two equal spans the later of the rows answers: a deeper node of a tree is written later. The
canvas's contract C6 no longer excuses a point on a shared edge, and its test has 260,000
cases (`canvas-test-run.txt`).

Tested after the fixes, `lower-run.txt`: 12 pages, 289,872 points, 0 differ.

### 7. The laws and the finite checks

Five laws are proved in `Scene.lean`. `#print axioms` prints `[propext, Quot.sound]` for four
of them and `[propext]` for the first.

| Law | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| `lowerAll_append` | The lowering is a map of lists: a page lowers call by call. |
| `pick_append` | A later call answers the pointer before an earlier one. |
| `box_holds` | A fill never vanishes: at a ratio of one or more its box holds a pixel. |
| `frameSides_pairwise` | The four sides of a frame share no pixel. |
| `edgeParts_pairwise` | The three parts of a tree's edge share no pixel. |

The last two are the painter's rule P4. The detail pass tested it on random cases. Here it is
a theorem of the lowered form.

Five finite checks run on the lowered pages (`lower-run.txt`).

| Check | Of | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every fill lies inside the page | 125 pages | 125 |
| Every cut is closed, and none opens inside another | 125 pages | 125 |
| A page moved by whole pixels lowers to the moved device calls | 125 pages | 125 |
| No two boxes of a graph share a cell | 17 graphs | 17 |
| Both texts of a box fit the box | 359 boxes | 359 |

The fourth check is the premise of the canvas's index (`canvas.h`: two boxes of one row do not
overlap). Until this round nothing checked it.

The observation that joins Lean and C is the stream of device calls. The statement is a
finite check on the fragment of finding 5, and not a theorem.

### 8. What the lowering does not hold yet

| Part | Where it stands | What landing it in Lean needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The camera at a zoom other than 1 | `canvas.c` | a zoom in the snap: `floor(v * zoom * ratio + 1/2)`. The steps are irrational, so the proof-side form needs a rational ladder or binary64. |
| The cursor, the fold of lines, the scroll | `view.c` | fields of the reader's state in `View` |
| The level of detail | `canvas.c` | a function of the zoom that chooses the calls |
| The width of a text of a proportional face | pango | the advances of a face as a table, and the kerning. Not measured here. |
| The key of a fill | nowhere | a key on each `Op`, carried to each `Dev` |

The last row is the tie to the code that the owner asked for. A pointer box has a key today,
and a fill has none. With a key on each call, one pixel names its node. The tables of the
earlier note then give its authored line and its printed span.

## Proposals (not rulings)

**A. The schema view.**

1. Generate for `Representation` what `Ty` has: the constructor tags, the arguments by sort,
   `ofLayer`, and a replacement at a path. One generic layer then serves the lines, the graph,
   the spans and each later view.
2. Hold the names of types as data in a module, as it holds the rows. A page and the printer
   can then write `Todo` where the record stands.
3. Give membership a located answer: a path into the value and the type, and a reason.
4. Draw a document's references as a graph with shared nodes. This is the open graph work of
   the earlier note.

**B. The lowering.**

1. Land the three forms as data, with `pageOps` and `lower` as functions. C keeps the replay,
   the window and the shaping of text.
2. Put a key on each call.
3. State the layout's laws as theorems of the two layouts: no two boxes share a cell, and a
   text fits its box. Today they are finite checks.
4. Decide the zoom ladder with the proofs in view: rational steps make the snap exact in
   integers.

**C. The place of the laws.** No concept of `docs/core/semantics.md` covers a view. Before a
law of the picture is stated in the tree, the owner rules one of two things. Either a view is
a tool, outside the gate, and its laws are tests. Or a view is a face of the language, with a
concept, claims and the gate.

**D. The two readings of "in the authoring API".**

1. A face beside the printer: a built program answers its pages and its device calls, as it
   answers its TypeScript module.
2. The viewer as a program of the language: a row asks the host to present a frame, and a row
   waits for an event. The window is the host, and a session of the viewer is a run with a
   journal. Not tested here.

**E. What the lowered form allows next.**

- A second target from the same list: SVG, PDF, and the terminal's characters.
- The difference of two lists: the boxes to repaint, and a regression test that names the call
  that changed. This round used it: a stream localised each defect at once.
- Motion between two lists, joined by key.

**Questions for the owner.**

1. Which reading of "in the authoring API" is meant: D.1, D.2 or both?
2. Is a view a face of the language with claims, or a tool (proposal C)?
3. Shall the name of a type be data (proposal A.2)?

Two small rules were decided in the probe, and the owner may overrule them. A box holds its
left and top edge and not its right and bottom edge. Of two equal spans the later of the rows
answers a click.

## What this does not establish

- No theorem joins Lean's lowering and the C painter. The agreement is a finite check of 125
  pages under 8 settings, on the fragment of finding 5.
- The five laws stand in a probe file. No registry claim holds them, and the axiom gate does
  not read the file.
- The stream fixes no pixel of a text. Two machines with other faces draw other glyphs.
- The lowering restates the page rules of `view.c` in Lean. It is a second statement of them,
  and `view.c` still holds the first.
- No person used the window in this round. The time of a lowering inside a frame loop is not
  measured: 168 ms is one batch of 125 pages in the interpreter.
- The schema pages show 5 types of one application. No schema with annotations, a filter
  group, a reference or a recursion is shown. No schema written in TypeScript is read.
- The span method reads the printer's text by its braces. It is tested on 25 nodes.
- The checks of the detail pass were not rerun.
